Traditional materials

Below are examples of traditional materials, their purpose and how they can benefit us.

Metals and alloys

A metal alloy is a mixture that's made by melting a base metal with one or more elements

Steel

Steel is a very strong material. Used in structural bridges and reinforcement bars, it can get rusty overtime

Aluminum

Lightweight material, used in aircraft bodywork, window frames and electronics, it's resistant to corrosion.

Copper

A highly conductive material, used in electrical wiring and plumbing.

Cast iron

Heavy and brittle, used in engine blocks, heavy machinery and piping.

Ceramics and glass

Regular glass

Transparent material, used as windows and bottles, has a set level of light passing. Unlike photochromic, it can't change its tint or heat transmission, it's different from photochromic.

Clay bricks

Heat resistant material, it's hard and used to build walls. Can crack under high levels of thermal shock or physical impact.

Concrete

A composite material made up with gravel or sand mixed with water and cement paste, very strong under compression but it can get cracks and overtime allowing moisture to get inside unlike bio cement.

Polymers

Polyethylene

Standard plastic material. Used in bottles, packaging and piping. Keeps it shape but it will degrade over a long period of time under the sun

Polyvinyl Chloride

Very rigid material, or can be flexible. Used in plumbing, window frames and cable insulation.

Natural rubber

Used specifically for tires, can also be used for sealing. Although it's elastic, standard rubber can't change its shape, its very stiff material.
