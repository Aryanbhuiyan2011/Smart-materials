Traditional materials

For this page, i'll be showing you all the different types of traditional materials, what they do and how they could benefit us.

Metals and alloys

Steel

Steel has very strong strength, it is used for structural frames, bridges and reinforcement bars, overtime it can rust and degrade.

Aluminum

it's very lightweight, it's used in aircraft bodies, window frames and electronics, it's corrosian resistant by the way.

Copper

It's a highly conductive metal used for electrical wiring and plumbing pipes

Cast iron

It's heavy brittle material that's used for engine blocks, heavy machinery and piping.

Ceramics and glass

Regular glass

It's transparent, it's used for windows and bottles, it allows a fixed amount of light through it and can't change it's tint or heat passage like photochromic, it's different.

Clay bricks

They're heat resistant, they're rigid building materials used for walls. They do crack under high thermal shock or physical impact

Concrete

It's a composite with gravel or sand mixed with water and cement paste. It's extremely strong in compression however it can naturally form cracks overtime that lets moisture in it unlike bio concrete.

Polymers

Polyethylene

It's just standard plastic that's used in bottles, packaging and piping. They maintain a fixed shape and break down over long time under the sun.

Polyvinyl Chloride

it's very rigid, or it could be flexibile. It's used in plumbing, window frames and cable insulation.

Natural rubber

It's used for tires in specific, it can also be used for seals too. While it is elastic, standard rubber can't change it's shape, it's very stiff.
